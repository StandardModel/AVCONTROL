### Assistant

That’s a fair question, Ron. **Price and sound quality don’t rise in lockstep**, especially when comparing an established manufacturer with a clone.

An established brand’s price includes engineering, quality control, dealer margins, warranty service, and long-term support. A clone can avoid much of the original development cost and sell through a much leaner operation. Those differences don’t necessarily translate into better sound in your system.

There are also several possible explanations for what you’re hearing:

- **System compatibility:** Output impedance and coupling capacitors interacting with your power amp’s input can affect bass response.
- **Condition:** Depending on its age and model, the Audio Research could have worn tubes or components needing attention.
- **Different sonic character:** Differences in frequency response or distortion can influence perceived presence. A stronger preference alone doesn’t tell us which circuit measures more accurately.

Since you’ve already taken care with listening levels, I wouldn’t dismiss your experience because the cheaper unit “shouldn’t” win.

**Which Audio Research model is it, and what power amplifier are you using?** That would help us distinguish a plausible electrical explanation from speculation.
